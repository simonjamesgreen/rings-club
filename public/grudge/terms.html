Who can use Grudge

You must be at least 13, and we ask for your date of birth when you join. If you are under 18, you need a parent or guardian's permission, and some features are switched off for you: private messages, nudges and open leagues with people you don't know. You can still play in leagues you are invited to and use the league chat. You need an Apple Account, and an Apple Watch for your activity to count.

Be decent: no tolerance for abuse

Grudge has a league chat (Banter), private messages and nudges. Banter between mates is the point. Abuse is not. You must not use Grudge to post or send anything that:

  • harasses, threatens, bullies or intimidates anyone;
  • is hateful or discriminatory, including about race, religion, sex, gender, sexuality, disability or age;
  • is sexual, or sexualises anyone, and in particular anything involving children;
  • encourages self-harm, violence or anything illegal;
  • is spam, a scam, or shares someone else's private information.

There is no tolerance for objectionable content or abusive users. We filter some abusive words automatically. You can report any message (long-press it) and block any player. We review reports within 24 hours, and we can remove content and suspend or permanently remove anyone who breaks these rules, without notice. League hosts can also remove players from their leagues.

Reports and complaints

To report content or a player, long-press the message in the app and choose Report, or email simonjamesgreen@gmail.com. If you think we got a decision wrong, for example your message was removed or your account was restricted unfairly, email the same address and we will look again. If you believe a child is at risk, contact the police.

Play fair

Scores come from your own Apple Watch activity. Don't fake activity, tamper with the app or its data, use someone else's device, or try to access other people's accounts or information. We can correct scores and remove players who cheat.

Exercise within your limits, rest when you are ill or injured (that's what rest days are for), and speak to a doctor before starting or stepping up exercise if you have any health concerns. You take part at your own risk.
